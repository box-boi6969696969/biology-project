The Birth of a Legend

During the Qing Dynasty's Daoguang period (1821-1850), Buddha Jumps Over the Wall emerged from the culinary innovation of Fuzhou's elite. Originally named "Fortune and Longevity Complete" (福寿全), the dish was created by a government official's wife to impress the Fujian Provincial Governor Zhou Lian.

Legends & Stories

Multiple tales explain how this legendary dish got its name

📖

The Scholar's Journey

A traveling scholar preserved food in a wine jar during his journey. When he arrived in Fuzhou, the aroma of his heated meal tempted a monk to jump over the monastery wall.

🏮

The Beggar's Discovery

Beggars collected leftover food in wine jars. A restaurant owner discovered their mixed leftovers created an incredibly aromatic stew, inspiring the refined version.

🎭

The Poet's Inspiration

At a literary gathering, guests were so impressed by the dish that one poet wrote: "The aroma spreads to neighboring homes, even Buddha would jump over the wall."

Cultural Significance

Buddha Jumps Over the Wall represents more than culinary excellence—it embodies Chinese cultural values of hospitality, craftsmanship, and the pursuit of harmony between ingredients and preparation methods.
